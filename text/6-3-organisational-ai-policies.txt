

## Following Organisational AI Policies and Governance Standards
Learn this interactivelyConcept Check|Build Coach

## What You Need to Know
The sanctioned workspace is the Claude workspace that your organisation provides. A personal account is an account that an individual holds for themselves. Shadow AI is work material in an AI tool that the organisation does not provide. The rule is short: use the workspace that your organisation provides. The rule sounds like etiquette. It has real reasons behind it. Understanding what the rule is based on lets you answer any version of the question without memorising a table.

This lesson uses one made-up example throughout: a minicab firm that provides Claude to its office staff. A new dispatcher's access has not arrived, and a hotel contract is due on Friday. She pastes the contract and the complaints log into her personal Claude account because it is quicker. The bookings manager discovers this.

## Why the Sanctioned Workspace Is Substance
When an organisation provides Claude, the arrangement governs how Anthropic handles the organisation's data. The organisation decides what happens to its data. Anthropic handles the data only on the organisation's instructions. In the Privacy Center's terms, the customer is Controller of the data that its users submit. Anthropic is Processor on the customer's behalf. The commercial training article states that by default Anthropic will not use inputs or outputs from commercial products to train its models. Administrators control the workspace's settings. On the Enterprise plan, the organisation sets custom retention timelines for its data. Users still delete their own chats from their visible history. The organisation controls the workspace's retention, but it does not control the deletion action. The agreement, the administration and the data handling all apply to the sanctioned workspace. The rule protects these three things. In the firm's workspace, the firm's agreement would cover the contract.

## Personal Accounts Are Different
The individual's own consumer relationship with Anthropic governs a personal account. The individual's own settings decide whether Anthropic uses the chats for model improvement. Consumer deletion and retention rules apply. The employer does not administer, see or govern any of it. The problem is not carelessness. The organisation's agreement and controls do not apply to the account. The dispatcher's own agreement now covers the hotel contract, not the firm's agreement. A paid personal plan with careful settings would change nothing, because the question is whose agreement governs the data.

## The Shadow-AI Response Has Two Halves
The response to a colleague's workaround has two halves. First, do not copy the workaround, however urgent the deadline is. Second, report the workaround to whoever owns the policy or the data. The disclosure has already happened, and someone with the authority to handle it needs to know. An unreported workaround also continues. The colleague's underlying problem is real: access has not arrived for work that is due. Only the organisation can fix this problem. When you report the workaround, the organisation learns that its access process is too slow. So reporting is not an accusation against a colleague. The bookings manager keeps her own work in the workspace. She tells the policy's owner about the disclosure and the late access. Asking the dispatcher to delete her chats or close her account does not undo the disclosure, because the contract has already left the firm.

## Policy Silence and Working Habits
When a policy is silent on a question, the question has not been answered. The professional action is to ask the policy's owner. This is the escalation that objective 6.2 teaches. It is an action, not a refusal. If the firm's policy says nothing about pasting complaints into Claude, the bookings manager asks the policy's owner first. The basic working habits are plain, and the exam can test them:

know which account and plan you work in

know who administers it

follow the rules for what may be uploaded

know how to report a problem

When policy conflicts with convenience, the policy takes priority. When the policy is genuinely wrong or too slow, ask its owner to change it rather than bypass it.

## In the exam
The scenario that appears often in the exam describes a colleague who uses a personal account for work material. The colleague does this because it is quicker or their access has not arrived. Candidates who treat the rule as etiquette lose marks to wrong options that are based on good intentions. The right answer does both halves, and candidates reliably miss the second half. Wrong options offer the first half alone, or deleting chats and closing the account as a remedy, or a paid personal plan with careful settings. The most common wrong option treats anything that the policy does not mention as allowed.
Boundary discipline
Objective 6.2 covers whether material may be uploaded at all. Domain 4 covers how to design a process. In this objective, you escalate to whoever owns the policy or the data, never to a technical specialist.

Anthropic publishes no guidance on what an organisational AI policy should contain. This objective is based on the exam guide's wording and on the documented product differences that underlie the sanctioned workspace.

## Exam Traps

Exam Trap
Fixing only half of the shadow-AI situation
Declining to copy the workaround while saying nothing leaves the disclosure unknown and the access gap unfixed, so the workaround continues. The right answer does both halves: stop the practice and raise it with whoever owns the policy or the data.

Exam Trap
Offering deletion as remediation for material already disclosed
Deleting conversations or closing the account is a step taken after the disclosure: the material has already left the organisation, and nobody with authority knows about it or the access gap. Stop the workaround and report both to whoever owns the policy or the data.

Exam Trap
Treating policy silence as permission
A policy that does not mention something has not answered the question. Assuming permission converts a gap in a document into an unauthorised disclosure; the right move is asking the policy owner, which is an action, not a refusal.

## Practice Scenario
The operations lead at a recycling social enterprise discovers that a colleague has been pasting the local authority contract and staff shift records into a personal free Claude account at home, because the organisation's licences have not been issued yet and the work is due. The enterprise's data rules keep its material out of personal accounts on any plan, and any slip must be reported. What should the operations lead do?
Option AAsk the colleague to delete the conversations and her account history, and confirm that the contract and the shift records are no longer held anywhere outside the organisation
Option BLet it continue until the licences arrive, since the colleague is careful about what she pastes and the work genuinely cannot wait
Option CMove the work onto his own personal account instead, since he pays for a plan with stricter data settings, closer to what the organisation will provide
Option DStop the personal-account work now, and tell whoever owns the policy about the disclosure and the licence delay so both get dealt with
