

## Evaluating Outputs for Accuracy and Completeness
Learn this interactivelyConcept Check|Build Coach

## What You Need to Know
Reviewing an output means checking what Claude wrote before anyone relies on it. A review asks two questions. Accuracy asks whether the specifics are right: numbers, names, dates, titles, quoted figures, and references to documents or rules. Completeness asks whether anything is missing. A review checks both against the source. The source is the material that Claude used to build the output.

This lesson uses one made-up example throughout: a bakery. Its owner attaches a flour supplier's new contract and asks Claude for a summary. Her business partner will use the summary to decide whether to sign. The summary reads well, but it could contain a wrong price per sack or omit a clause.

## The Reviewer's Stance
Treat every Claude output as a good first draft that can contain errors stated with confidence. The Help Center says why: Claude "can occasionally produce responses that are incorrect or misleading", and users "should not rely on Claude as a singular source of truth". Fluency and confidence are properties of the writing, not evidence about the facts. Claude writes fluently whether it is right or wrong, so an output that reads beautifully has passed no check. The bakery summary would read just as well with a wrong price.

Ownership decides who does the checking. The professional who sends Claude's work owns it. That professional is accountable for it to their team, their members or their funder. Review is the step that changes a draft into work that you can accept responsibility for. Review is not an optional extra for cautious people.

## Accuracy: Are the Specifics Right?
Check the specifics first, in order of consequence. Start with the details that would be most costly or embarrassing if they were wrong. These are usually the figure that a decision depends on, the name at the top of the letter, and the date of the deadline. A draft with twenty accurate sentences and one wrong subtotal is a wrong draft if the meeting decides on that subtotal. At the bakery, the price per sack comes first, because the partner decides on it.

## Completeness: Is Anything Missing?
Completeness is harder to check, because the evidence is absent rather than wrong. A fluent summary can omit a section, a theme or a caveat, and nothing in the prose shows the gap. So check the output against the source itself, never against your memory of the source. Follow the source's own structure: its sections, funds, themes or line items. Confirm that each element is either in the output or excluded on purpose. If you read the output alone and accept it, you send it with the gaps still in it. The bakery owner ticks each clause in the contract against the summary.

Check accuracy first every time. A wrong specific is the most likely failure, and it is the quickest to catch. Check completeness second, because that check needs the source open anyway.

## Verify Against the Input
When Claude builds an output from attached material, the first check is the attachment. The attachment is already in the conversation, and it takes minutes to compare the two. Anthropic's prompting guidance gives the same advice for long documents: ask Claude to quote the relevant parts of the source before it does the task. In the chat, ask Claude where in the attachment each figure or claim came from. Then spot-check the claims that matter against those locations. An output whose claims trace to the source is checkable in minutes. Do not assume that an output is correct when you cannot trace its claims. The bakery owner asks where the price came from, then reads that clause.

Asking Claude whether it is sure is not a check. The same model that made an error will repeat it with the same confidence, because no new information enters the check. A real check compares the output with its source.

## In the exam
This domain has a larger share of the exam than any other domain, and every objective in it depends on the reviewer's stance. The certification's central claim is that its holder can be trusted with what Claude produces. The holder earns that trust at the review stage, not at the prompting stage. When the stance is right, the other skills in the domain follow from it: spotting failure types, validating claims, knowing when to escalate. The exam treats self-reported confidence as a wrong answer, never as a check.
The register exemplar
The guide's Domain 2 sample question tests this objective. Claude summarises a regulation and cites a specific subsection. The right answer is to verify that citation against the official text before the compliance team sees it. The named wrong answers are trusting Claude's expressed confidence and rewording the summary to sound more formal. Diligence matters more than confidence, and polish is never a substitute for correctness.

## Exam Traps

Exam Trap
Sending the output because it reads well or because Claude expressed high confidence
Fluency and confidence are properties of the writing, not evidence about the facts, so neither has checked anything. Compare the output against the source material before anyone relies on it.

Exam Trap
Asking Claude to confirm its own work and treating a confident yes as review
No new information enters the check: the same model that made an error will repeat it with the same confidence. Compare the output with its source, or ask Claude where in the source each claim came from and spot-check those locations.

Exam Trap
Verifying one named figure when part of the source may have been dropped
Accuracy checking alone misses the second review question. When the source had more in it than the output shows, review each part of the source's own structure for completeness, not just the details that you noticed.

## Practice Scenario
A scout group treasurer has Claude draft the annual financial summary for the AGM from the group's accounts spreadsheet. The draft reads perfectly, but she must be sure it is right and leaves nothing out, without simply trusting what Claude says. What should the treasurer do before circulating it?
Option ARework the summary into more formal accounting language so that it passes close examination by members at the meeting
Option BCirculate it as it stands: the figures look plausible, the formatting is professional, and the AGM pack deadline is close
Option CAsk Claude whether it is confident the summary is accurate and complete, then circulate the document once Claude says it is
Option DCheck each stated figure against the spreadsheet, then review the list of funds the accounts track to confirm every fund appears in the summary
